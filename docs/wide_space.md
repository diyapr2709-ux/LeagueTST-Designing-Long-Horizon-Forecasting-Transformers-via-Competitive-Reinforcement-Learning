# Wide design space (amendment A16, paper stage)

Generated by scripts/wide_space.py from leaguetst.search.wide_space and the E1 timings in results/ledger.jsonl. Do not edit by hand.

## Why the current space is narrow

PatchTST's flatten head has d_model x n_patches x T weights, seven times more with channel mixing. Over the four horizons it holds 79 to 97% of PatchTST/42's parameters on ETTh1 and 48 to 70% on ILI. The head grows with width, so under the PatchTST/42 cap at all eight settings only widths 16 and 32 and channel-independent configs fit, and the set is 74 architectures crossed with 32 training settings (docs/feasible_set.md). Within that set the head holds 63 to 99% of the parameters on ETTh1 and 13 to 87% on ILI, where a coarse patching of the 104-step lookback leaves as few as 5 patches.

## The wide space

`leaguetst/models/wide.py` adds three fields to the nine of `ForecasterConfig`:

* `input_len`, the lookback: ETTh1 96, 192, 336, 512; ILI 52, 104, 156.
* `head_type`: `flatten` (PatchTST's head), `pooled` (mean over patches, then d_model -> T), `lowrank` (flatten -> r -> T) and `shared_linear` (one d_model -> T map per patch, then a learned mix over patches for each output step).
* `head_rank`, the r of the low-rank head: 4, 8, 16, all below the shortest horizon (24), so the head is low rank at every setting.

With the flatten head and today's fields, `WideTST` builds the same modules in the same order as `PatchTST`: same parameter count, same weights and same forecasts under the same seed (tests/test_wide_space.py). The grid crosses today's 30,720 configs with the 6 head choices and the lookbacks: 737,280 configs on ETTh1 and 552,960 on ILI. Parameter counts are closed form (`leaguetst.search.wide_space.parameter_parts`); the tests check them against models instantiated on the meta device for 600 random configs and every head at the largest shape, and against `patchtst_parameters` on all 30,720 configs at all eight settings.

Feasibility uses the caps of `leaguetst.search.feasible.BUDGETS`. A config is feasible at a setting when its parameter count is at most the cap. "All four horizons" is the set a winner must fit to be retrained at every horizon of its dataset (E6). The last set keeps each dataset's default lookback (336, 104) and asks for all eight settings, the definition of today's 2,368; with the flatten head the wide grid reproduces every count of docs/feasible_set.md exactly.

## What the cap now allows

* Size. The four-horizon sets hold 284,480 configs (8,890 architectures) on ETTh1 and 171,488 (5,359) on ILI; the eight-setting set holds 53,088 (1,659), against 2,368 (74) today.
* Width 64 becomes feasible at every horizon through the cheaper heads: 31,808 configs on ETTh1 and 2,976 on ILI. Width 128 is feasible only at ETTh1 T=192 and above, and width 256 only at ETTh1 T=720. That limit is the encoder, not the head: the smallest backbone at width 128 (one layer, ff ratio 1) has 101,632 weights, above the ETTh1 T=96 cap (81,728) and every ILI cap (at most 57,628); at width 256 it has 399,872.
* Channel mixing becomes feasible: 126,976 of the ETTh1 four-horizon configs and 75,072 of ILI's, against none in today's set.
* The flatten head stays in the space and keeps today's configs, so a search over the wide space can still return a PatchTST.

## Cost model

Fitted to the steady epochs (2 onward) of 6,036 E1 ledger rows on the NVIDIA A10G (ETTh1 2,011, ILI 4,025), four worker processes per GPU. Each feature counts one epoch (leaguetst/search/cost_model.py):

| Feature | Coefficient |
|---|---|
| step (training steps + 1/3 of validation steps) | 2.421 ms |
| step x encoder layer | 2.719 ms |
| matmul work, per TFLOP | 5.028 s |
| elementwise work, per 10^9 elements | 0.0431 s |

The matmul coefficient implies 0.20 TFLOPS per worker with four workers sharing the GPU, far below its peak: at widths 16 and 32 the matrices are too small to keep it busy.

Fit quality. Held-out rows are scored by a model fitted without them, and the rows of one config (its seeds) are always held out together:

R^2 on a subset with little spread in epoch time is low even when the relative errors are small; the relative errors and the totals are the numbers that matter for a price. The cross-dataset rows show how far the fit carries: ILI epochs (16 windows per batch) do not predict ETTh1 epochs (128), which is why both datasets are in the fit.

Accounting check on the fitted rows: their wall time divided by four workers is 10.30 GPU-hours; epochs run times the corrected predicted epoch time, plus each row's overhead, gives 10.28.

## Price of enumerating the wide space

One seed per config at the table setting, on an A10G at $1.42 per GPU-hour (CPU and memory included), 4 workers per GPU. Each config is charged the mean epochs run by its dataset's E1 rows (the 30-epoch cap when there are none) plus the mean per-config overhead. The fit minimises relative error per row, which leaves the long ETTh1 epochs under-predicted on average, so each predicted epoch is multiplied by the dataset's measured over predicted total on held-out rows (the correction column). "Over 5 GB" counts the configs whose rough activation memory exceeds a quarter of the A10G's 24 GB less each worker's CUDA context; they need fewer workers per GPU but are not charged more, since a config that large keeps the GPU busy on its own. The FLOP floor is the matmul work alone at the A10G's FP32 peak (31.2 TFLOPS, TF32 off), a bound no fit can undercut.

Prices are per seed; E1 trains ILI at three seeds. The fitted rows have widths 16 and 32, the flatten head, channel-independent inputs and the default lookback, so prices for wider, mixing or long-lookback configs extrapolate the fit. The per-TFLOP cost was measured on small matrices, which use the GPU least efficiently, so for widths 64 and 128 it is more likely to overstate than understate the time. The measured epoch times include contention between the four workers. The memory estimate counts every elementwise output of a batch at four bytes, more than autograd keeps, so it overstates the configs over 5 GB.
