## 4. Framing a negative or mixed result as a contribution

The paper is a pre-registered negative result with an audited benchmark. Its contributions, in the order a reviewer will accept them:

1. **The benchmark and its failure modes.** Two 2,368-config PatchTST tables at matched parameter budgets (3 seeds on ILI, per-window validation errors for every entry, A1), a live-retrain validity check (E3b) that fails on ILI by a winner's curse and passes on ETTh1 but cannot order the top 20, a plateau of 50 to 59 configs within one seed SD of the best, and tables that disagree across datasets (Spearman -0.21). These explain why search methods cannot separate at 25 to 100 full-run equivalents, and they are results about tabular NAS benchmarks, not about this project's method.
2. **Pre-registration with a public amendment trail**, independent re-scores, and failure handling fixed before results (A25-A30, AB1-AB4). Few HPO-with-LLM papers do this; say so once, then show the deviations appendix.
3. **Failure analysis of the RL architect**: GRPO learned the source reward (AB-L, with the liberal-test caveat), the policies acquired a source-table preference (RevIN off) that is wrong on the target, and the league added nothing over no league or uniform rivals in the controller (AB-C, E3). This separates "did not learn" from "did not transfer", which is the paper's most useful sentence for practitioners.
4. **The referee (RQ2)**: a cheap learned ranker beats epoch-k screening on both tables, draws its skill from the config fields, and is not worse on the architect's proposals than on matched random configs (exploratory margin). Honest and reusable.
5. **Negative evidence against LLM-driven search in small discrete spaces**, consistent with the budget-matched null in the literature (docs/related_work.md [1.5]) and with TPE and SMAC leading pure LLM methods in [1.4].

Do not frame as: a new method, a competitive league, transformer design, or a win on ILI validation.

### Abstract skeleton (no numbers; every quantity comes from scripts/report.py and the generators it names)

> We ask whether an LLM architect fine-tuned with GRPO against a league of rivals configures PatchTST-style forecasters better than random search, regularized evolution, TPE, ASHA and the same LLM without reinforcement learning, at equal training epochs. We build two tabular benchmarks of every configuration within the PatchTST/42 parameter budget on ETTh1 and ILI, with multi-seed entries and per-window validation errors, pre-register every endpoint, test and amendment, and re-score every result independently. On fresh training seeds no method beats random search; an in-table win for TPE is a winner's curse that a live retrain exposes. The transferred GRPO policy is worse than warm-started TPE and no better than a portfolio or an untrained policy, although it learned its source reward: it acquired a normalisation preference that does not transfer. A learned referee ranks configurations better than early-epoch screening. We release the tables, the pre-registration trail and the failure analysis.

(About 140 words.)
